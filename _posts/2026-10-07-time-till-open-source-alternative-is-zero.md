I wrote a blog post four years ago called [Time till Open Source Alternative](/time-till-open-source-alternative.html). And the thesis was that it is becoming quicker and quicker to see an open source alternative to established products. Back then, AI was not on my radar, as for everyone else in the industry, but I knew the trend toward a descending time till open source was going to persist.

My assumption would have been that it would take a few weeks or a few days to make an open source alternative to a proprietary product, but the reality is that if we look at the new numbers, it has now gone to **zero**. As the latest example, it took zero days for Grokbot to get an open source alternative called OpenBossBot. (Obviously, the definition of time to open source alternative is the genesis of the proprietary software in relation to the genesis of the open source software. Obviously, it takes some while for the software to mature.)

So it is realistic to assume that this trend won't go back. We are solidly now operating under the assumption that any proprietary software release will be nearly instantly made an open source alternative. Frankly, this process itself can also be made automatic. People on the internet can just have bots that listen to product announcements and kickstart an open source alternative as soon as possible. That is all viable and feasible today with the tools we have. And the tools themselves are just gonna get better and better.

So I wanna talk a little bit about the implications of this for us. What does it mean in a world where any proprietary software launches under the assumption that they will compete with a free and open source alternative?

## Demonetization

First implication is demonetization. When I was building many versions of the Scuttlebutt ecosystem, I was explicitly aiming for demonetizing social networks, in particular Facebook and Instagram and the likes. My motivation was that some software is so foundational and so essential for modern life that it becomes unjust to charge for such software when it can be trivially offered as open source.

The other angle to view this situation is economic. When supply explodes and demand is kept constant, then prices go down. That is another argument for demonetization of software. Guillermo Rauch, the CEO of Vercel, recently tweeted that all software in the future will become free. And I think this also hints towards the trend of demonetization.

## Remixable and personalized

The other implication that zero time till open source alternative gives us is that software is becoming remixable and more personal, personalized and customized. This can be seen clearly in the recompilation and decompilation communities, where people are blending different source codes of games to remix games, essentially. Something similar can happen with productivity software and other kinds of software, where you can just more fluidly configure software or customize it to how you like. You can pick ideas from other software, you can fluidly adapt it to your needs.

We must also remember that outside of the techie circles, people are not creating their own personalized software, and that's not going to happen. We are not going to see people building their own software in the same way that every person has a mobile phone. What I mean is that the normal person won't be creating their own software. It just simply demands a certain level of interest in software, to build software. Obviously, the new class of so-called vibe coders create a sort of eternal September effect, and a lot of new people are now empowered with AI to vibe code the software that they need. I'm not talking about that. I'm just saying that the future of fluid and customized software does not necessarily mean that everybody will customize software for themselves.

## Software as content

At the end of the day, software is just a kind of content. It's not much different than images or videos or text. So the same dynamics that AI forces on content production in entertainment and video and memes and things like that will be seen in software as well. Software will become more mundane, more abundant, cheaper, and in many ways, unsurprising.

I say this not as a prediction of the future. I'm just saying this as the implications of the trend that we already have. I think if I were to say these things back in 2022, it would have classified as a prediction of the future, but now with all of the evidence we see, this is just the new normal. And we have to adjust our expectations.
